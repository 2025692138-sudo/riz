ABOUT

NISSAN GT-R

The Skyline was first created by Prince Motor Company in 1957 before the company merged with Nissan. In 1964, the Prince Skyline GT was introduced with a more powerful 2.0L inline-6 engine, marking the beginning of Skyline's performance heritage. The GT-R name stands for Gran Turismo Racing, representing Nissan's focus on speed, technology, and motorsport. The first official Skyline GT-R (PGC10) was launched in 1969 and became famous after winning 33 consecutive races in Japan. In 1972, the KPGC110 GT-R was discontinued due to the global energy crisis, with only 197 units produced. The GT-R returned in 1989 with the legendary R32, followed by the R33 (1995), R34 (1999), and the modern R35 (2007). Over the years, Nissan and NISMO (Nissan Motorsport) continued improving the GT-R with advanced technology, making it one of the world's most iconic Japanese performance cars.

NISSAN LEGACY

HISTORY OF THE GT-R

From the first Skyline in 1957 to today's legendary R35 GT-R.

1957

Skyline Introduced

The Skyline was first introduced by Prince Motor Company in 1957 as a luxury sedan. It quickly became known for its reliability and engineering. This model laid the foundation for the future Skyline series before Prince merged with Nissan.

1964

Prince Skyline GT

In 1964, Prince introduced the Skyline GT, powered by a 2.0L inline-six engine. The car was developed for motorsport and competed in the Japanese Grand Prix, marking the beginning of Skyline's racing heritage.

1969

First Skyline GT-R

The first Skyline GT-R (PGC10) was launched in 1969. Equipped with the legendary S20 2.0L inline-six engine, it dominated Japanese touring car racing and achieved 33 consecutive victories, earning the GT-R a reputation as a high-performance sports car.

1973

Production Ends

Production of the original GT-R ended in 1972/1973 after the release of the KPGC110 "Kenmeri" GT-R. Due to the global oil crisis and stricter emissions regulations, only 197 units were produced before the GT-R was discontinued.

1989

R32 Return of the GT-R

After a 16-year absence, Nissan revived the GT-R with the R32 Skyline GT-R. It featured the powerful RB26DETT twin-turbo engine and the advanced ATTESA E-TS all-wheel-drive system. The R32 dominated motorsport and became famous worldwide with the nickname "Godzilla."

1995

Skyline GT-R R33

The R33 GT-R was launched in 1995 as the successor to the R32. It offered improved handling, better aerodynamics, and the upgraded ATTESA E-TS Pro AWD system. The R33 became the first production car to complete the Nürburgring Nordschleife in under 8 minutes, proving its world-class performance.

1999

Skyline GT-R R34

The R34 GT-R arrived in 1999 with a shorter, more aggressive design and the same legendary RB26DETT twin-turbo engine. It introduced an innovative 5.8-inch multifunction display that showed real-time performance data such as turbo boost and engine temperatures. Today, the R34 is widely regarded as one of the most iconic Japanese sports cars ever built.

2007 - Present

Nissan GT-R R35

The R35 GT-R was introduced in 2007 as a standalone model, no longer part of the Skyline family. Powered by the 3.8L VR38DETT twin-turbo V6 engine, it combines advanced technology, exceptional performance, and continuous improvements from NISMO, making it one of the world's most respected supercars.
